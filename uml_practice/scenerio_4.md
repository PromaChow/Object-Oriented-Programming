A bank wants a new system for its customers. A customer has a name, a national ID, a phone number and an address. A customer can have many accounts.

The bank has two kinds of accounts. A savings account gives interest every month. A current account does not give interest, but it allows an overdraft, so the customer can take out more money than the balance, up to a limit. Both accounts have an account number, a balance and an open date.

The customer can deposit money, withdraw money and check the balance. The customer can also transfer money to another account. Each action creates a transaction. A transaction has an ID, a date, an amount and a type. The system keeps all transactions in a list for each account.

Some rules are important. A savings account cannot go below zero. A current account cannot go below the overdraft limit. If the customer tries to break a rule, the system must show an error message and stop the action.

The bank also has employees. A bank teller helps customers at the desk. A bank manager can open and close accounts. Both have a name and an employee number.

Each account belongs to one branch. A branch has a name and an address. One branch has many accounts.

At the end of each month, the system adds interest to all savings accounts. Show this as a method in the class diagram.
